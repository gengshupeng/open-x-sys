# Open-X-Sys: let everyone create a Sys of their own

[中文](OPEN-X-SYS-DRAFT.zh-CN.md)

> **Design draft.** OpenXSys already provides a recursive natural-language runtime, runnable system instances, relation and capability models, user-owned continuity, and adapters connecting AI tools with external systems. This draft defines the next opening: how anyone can create, own, connect, and evolve a Sys on that common ground.

## In one sentence

**OpenXSys opens common ground from which anyone can create, own, and evolve a Sys for something they care about.**

```text
OpenXSys + your purpose, understanding, methods, tools, and reality
                                  ↓
                             your Sys
```

The `X` is a place open to everyone. It may be a line of work, a project, a field, an industry, a creative practice, a community, or something new that has not yet been named.

## The three parts of the name

| Part | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Open** | People can understand, own, change, connect, share, and fork their systems. Openness begins with the right to create, not merely access to source code. |
| **X** | Any person, subject, domain, capability, or shared purpose can occupy this place. The OpenXSys project does not fill X in advance. |
| **Sys** | It is not a prompt or a one-time answer. It is a system that can understand a subject, organize capability, make works, receive feedback, and keep evolving. |

Open-X-Sys is therefore an open way to generate systems. OpenCreatorSys, OpenLearnSys, OpenResearchSys, or OpenCitySys may become examples created by the project or community; what matters is that anyone can build a Sys suited to their own world and use their own name for it.

## Who can create what

- **Personal Sys:** make a person's learning, creating, research, work, or life into a system that can keep growing.
- **Project Sys:** bring a project's purpose, understanding, capabilities, works, relations, and handoffs into one continuing system.
- **Domain Sys:** organize a field's effective methods, knowledge, tools, data, and practices into a system an AI can use.
- **Team and community Sys:** let independently owned systems collaborate around a shared purpose while preserving rights and boundaries.
- **Public Sys:** open valuable understanding, capabilities, and works so that others can adopt, test, adapt, and extend them.

A Sys can remain private or be public. It can run independently or compose with other systems into a higher-level whole. Its creators and owners always decide whether it remains private or becomes public.

## Growing from the common ground of OpenXSys

A Sys receives common ground from OpenXSys:

- use systems to understand systems, and systems to build systems;
- let people speak naturally while AI carries the complexity;
- keep the process free while grounding judgment in systems science, evidence, and real feedback;
- make understanding reach people as works, choices, actions, or new capability;
- keep each system with its real owner, readable, correctable, portable, and terminable;
- measure growth by changed future behavior.

It then grows distinct parts from its own world:

| The Sys owns | What it may contain |
| --- | --- |
| **Purpose and subject** | Who it exists for, what it seeks to change, and which outcomes truly matter. |
| **Domain understanding** | Key concepts, subject models, evidence, unknowns, disputes, and change. |
| **Methods and practices** | Effective questions, judgments, routes, conditions, and experience from the field. |
| **Capabilities and tools** | Models, Skills, code, data, devices, people, and the relations among them. |
| **Works and experience** | The results this system is especially suited to make, and how people understand and use them. |
| **Boundaries and rights** | Authoritative state, permissions, risk, responsibility, correction, and exit. |
| **Feedback and evolution** | How effectiveness is known, and when the system updates, differentiates, combines, or retires. |

Common ground lets these systems understand and connect with one another. Their distinct parts keep them from collapsing into one template.

## Natural language should be the creation entry

In the future, creating a Sys should begin with a real sentence, not with filling a framework or copying a directory:

> Build a Sys for my independent research practice.

> Turn the product methods our team has validated into a Sys we can use together.

> I want to create an open system for walking in this city.

The AI first helps the creator understand the subject, purpose, boundary, and conditions already present. It then organizes the understanding, capabilities, tools, works, and feedback that are actually needed. File structures and machine indexes emerge as needed; people continue to understand, change, and run the system in natural language.

## How an open ecosystem can grow

```text
Someone creates a Sys of their own
                  ↓
Run, validate, and evolve it in real work
                  ↓
Keep it private, or open the shareable parts
                  ↓
Others adopt, adapt, connect, or fork it
                  ↓
New Sys and higher-level systems emerge
                  ↓
Real results return to the original system and the commons
```

This path may gradually grow two shared spaces:

- **Sys Hub:** a place to discover and understand public Sys. It helps people see what a Sys is for, where it fits, what it depends on, and who maintains it. It does not monopolize the right to create.
- **Systems Commons:** an open commons for systems, methods, practices, capabilities, and works. Shared material preserves provenance, conditions, versions, and lineage so others can truly continue it.

The value of an open ecosystem is not the number of names it lists. It is whether people can create capability that did not exist before and let that capability keep growing across systems.

## Relation to the current release

The main line of `0.1.0-alpha.1` remains making OpenXSys work as a complete system-level Skill inside the AI tool a person already uses: see through, think clearly, make it real, connect, grow, and get stronger with use.

The current architecture already lays foundations for Open-X-Sys: runnable system instances, natural-language entries, user ownership, relations, capabilities, adapters, semantic indexes, composition, and evolution. The present work is to test and strengthen those foundations across more real subjects and AI tools. Formal inheritance, interoperability, discovery, and commons designs will be derived further from Sys that people actually create.

For now, this draft does three things:

1. keeps the open direction of OpenXSys visible in the project story;
2. gives people who want to create a Sys a shared place to discuss and experiment;
3. lets future design grow from the runtime foundations validated in this release.

## Questions to answer before formal design

- What is the minimum shared meaning that lets a Sys remain free in form?
- Is the relation between OpenXSys and a Sys inheritance, composition, connection, or several at once?
- How does an AI discover, select, compose, or help create the right Sys from ordinary language?
- When several Sys act together, how are conflicts of purpose, method, permission, and authoritative state resolved?
- How should version, provenance, dependencies, conditions, validation, and fork lineage be expressed?
- How does interoperability create real effects instead of matching file formats or adding another line to a diagram?
- How can a Sys Hub and Systems Commons remain open while making quality, risk, and maintenance visible?

Architecture discussions, real works, and working Sys will answer these questions together, allowing formal agreements to grow naturally from the relations an open ecosystem truly needs.
